# Locally frozen design for an online matching comparison

Protocol frozen locally on 16 November 2026 before the first full evaluation. This is an independent implementation and controlled benchmark of a component from Bullinger, Romen and Schlenga, *The Power of Matching for Online Fractional Hedonic Games*, SODA 2026. [Paper and Algorithm 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.06163v2). The objective is aggregate fractional hedonic welfare. This is separate from the authors' stability results for additively separable games.

## Question and estimand

Does the fractional-matching component improve realised online welfare relative to the otherwise identical integral ablation under uniformly random arrival? The primary endpoint is the paired mean difference of each graph's average welfare divided by its exact unrestricted offline fractional hedonic welfare, where averaging is across orders and internal policy draws. The primary group is 48 fixed-seed independent signed triangular-cluster instances at size nine. A positive primary result requires a graph-resampling 95% lower endpoint above zero and a mean improvement of at least one percentage point. Other cohorts are transfer or diagnostic tests. No fitting or tuning uses those outcomes.

## Comparators and test design

- Implement the paper's random-arrival Algorithm 1 at k=3, with the half-integral fractional prefix optimum and its prescribed availability coin flips. Obtain the prefix optimum by symmetrising an exact maximum-weight directed assignment. Use independent symmetric tie marks that depend on the seen vertex set and graph replicate only, never availability or arrival order. Use separate availability and partner-selection draws at each step.
- Implement the paper's Appendix B.3 integral substitution with the same waiting phase, budget, information and availability rule. Use the same primary graph/order/seeds as the fractional variant.
- Compare both with immediate heaviest positive available pair and immediate greedy coalition placement by largest marginal welfare. These are practical reference algorithms without the same competitive guarantee. An exact offline matching and exact unrestricted offline coalition optimum provide clearly labelled ceilings.
- Use 32 random replicates per graph. The policy sees weights only between agents that have appeared. Use 48 graphs each of signed triangular modules, signed independent edges, and unequal dense compartments. Include a repeated unit-triangle diagnostic that identifies when the prefix optima differ, and nonuniform high-degree-first arrivals on the triangular family as a stress outside the theorem.
- Use an independently sourced SNAP ego-Facebook graph as a *topology proxy* if archived data are obtainable. Select 48 disjoint panels by fixed-seed breadth-first expansion to nine vertices, with neighbour ties randomised. Map observed edges to +2 and missing edges to −1. These are assigned test weights and do not measure human preferences. The social graph is a single dataset, the panels need not be independent populations, and no source node IDs or edges enter the repository. Report a conditional resampling interval and degree/density summaries.
- Preserve all effects, including family-specific false wins. Recompute with tie and seed hashes. Use 5,000 paired bootstrap draws across graph instances. Key audit checks are half-integrality, exact feasibility and consistency with enumerated small fractional matching structures and independent integral matching and partition solvers. Offline partition welfare is a reference denominator, not observed future information available to any online comparator.
